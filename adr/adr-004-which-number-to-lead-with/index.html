    ADR-004: Lead with the blanket cut cost, not the free volume¶

    Status: accepted Date: 2026-10-20

    Context¶

    The tool produces two headline candidates and the obvious one is wrong.

    Free volume is the share of a rule's alerts removable without losing a single detection. It is the number a SOC lead wants, it is intuitive, and it is what the first version of this README led with.

    Blanket cut cost is the share of true positives lost if a flat percentage of every rule's volume is removed. It is less intuitive and it is the number that actually refutes an uncosted tuning claim.

    The benchmark across three corpus sizes exposed the problem:

    Corpus Free volume Cost of halving
    50,000 alerts 17.3 percent 16.8 percent
    500,000 alerts 6.5 percent 15.6 percent
    2,000,000 alerts 2.7 percent 16.2 percent

    Free volume moved by a factor of six across the same stream. The cost of halving barely moved at all.

    Why free volume is unstable¶

    It is an order statistic of the minimum. Free volume is determined entirely by where the single lowest scoring true positive in the window happens to sit: every alert below it is removable, and nothing below it is. A longer window contains more detections, so it contains a lower worst one, so the figure shrinks. Isolated on a single rule with the separation and true positive rate held fixed:

    Window True positives Separation Free volume
    5,000 20 0.802 11.9 percent
    20,000 80 0.816 7.5 percent
    100,000 400 0.845 4.7 percent
    400,000 1,600 0.848 2.6 percent

    Separation is stable to three decimal places. Free volume falls by a factor of four and would keep falling, because the minimum of a sample has no limit other than the support of the distribution.

    This is not a bug in the computation. It is the statistic answering exactly the question it was asked, and the question containing a hidden dependence on how long you watched.

    Options considered¶

    1. Lead with free volume anyway. Rejected once the benchmark showed it moving by a factor of six on the same stream. A headline figure that depends on the export window is a figure a reader will quote in one meeting and be contradicted on in the next.
    2. Drop free volume. Rejected: it is the right answer to "what can we remove today", which is a real question, and it is correct for the window it was computed on.
    3. Report free volume at a fixed window. Rejected as a false fix. Fixing the window makes the figure comparable between runs while leaving it arbitrary, and hides the dependence rather than disclosing it.
    4. Lead with the blanket cut cost, keep free volume with its caveat. Chosen.

    Decision¶

    The README, the report and the badge lead with the cost of halving volume. Free volume stays in the report, in the tiles rather than the headline, and the window dependence is stated wherever it appears.

    Consequences¶

    The headline is now the number that does the work this tool was built for. "We cut alert volume in half" is the claim; "that costs 15.6 percent of your detections" is the reply, and it is a reply that holds whether the analysis ran on a week or a quarter.

    Free volume keeps its interval, which already communicated some of this: on the 500,000 alert corpus the interval on one rule runs from 1.1 to 13.9 percent, which is a reader's clue that the point estimate is soft. The interval captures sampling variation at a fixed window, though, not the window dependence itself, so the caveat is written out rather than left to the interval.

    A test asserts the direction: free volume must not increase as the window grows on a stream with fixed properties. If a change ever makes free volume look stable, something has been smoothed over.

    The honest framing for anyone reading the output: free volume says what is removable from this export. The blanket cut cost says what tuning costs. Only the second one is a property of the detections rather than of the window.
